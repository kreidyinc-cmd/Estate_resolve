Finding a bank account, investment or other asset after probate has been granted or an estate has been distributed does not necessarily mean the asset is lost or that probate must be repeated. The executor or administrator may need to deal with that asset and update the estate records before making any further payment to beneficiaries.

## Does a late-discovered asset still belong to the estate?

An asset owned by the deceased at death generally remains relevant to the estate even if it was discovered after the other assets were distributed. First establish what has been found and whether it belonged to the deceased. A jointly held account, pension death benefit or insurance payment may need different treatment; finding a provider's name alone does not prove that the estate can claim the money.

Ask the asset holder to confirm the ownership, the value at the date of death and any interest or other income arising since then. Keep its response with the estate accounts so you can explain how the new asset was dealt with.

## What should the executor do first?

1. **Record the discovery.** Note the account or policy reference, how it came to light, who holds it and what remains uncertain. Keep copies of relevant correspondence.
2. **Contact the asset holder.** Explain that you are acting for the estate and ask what identification and evidence of authority it needs before it can disclose information or release funds.
3. **Review the estate position.** Check the date-of-death value, any income since death, outstanding debts, expenses and the distributions already made. Avoid treating a possible match as money available to distribute until it has been confirmed.

The holder may ask for the Grant, identification and other estate documents. Requirements depend on the asset and the institution, so ask before sending originals or assuming the earlier paperwork will be enough.

## Is another probate application needed?

Not always. In England and Wales, an existing Grant may be sufficient for a newly identified asset, but the holder will tell you what it requires to release it. Procedures differ in Scotland and Northern Ireland. If your authority is unclear, or further confirmation appears necessary, seek advice for the relevant jurisdiction before taking the next step.

## Could tax or earlier distributions need attention?

A newly discovered asset can change the estate's reported value. Depending on what was previously reported, the executor may need to correct information given to HMRC and consider whether more Inheritance Tax is due. Interest, dividends or gains arising after death can raise separate tax questions. An appropriately qualified adviser can help establish what, if anything, needs reporting or paying in the circumstances.

If beneficiaries have already been paid, update the estate accounts and check how the newly recovered asset should be allocated under the will or intestacy rules. An additional distribution may be possible after relevant liabilities, tax and expenses are dealt with. Do not assume that the amount can simply be divided in the same proportions as an earlier payment without checking the beneficiaries' entitlements.

## Should you check for other missing assets?

One forgotten asset may point to gaps in the deceased's records. Review statements and correspondence for other accounts, investments, pensions or liabilities, and follow up with known providers.
