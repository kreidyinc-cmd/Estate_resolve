**No, a professional financial asset search is not automatically compulsory for every estate.** If the records are complete and you can confirm the relevant holdings with known providers, direct enquiries may be enough. A wider search becomes worth considering when there are credible gaps: missing statements, several previous addresses, uncertain pensions or signs of an account you cannot identify. The right choice depends on what remains unknown, the estate's circumstances and the scope of any proposed search.

## What is a financial asset search?

It is a set of enquiries intended to identify possible financial holdings connected with a person who has died. Depending on the provider and agreed scope, it may cover selected banks, building societies, savings providers or other institutions. It does not replace checking the deceased's papers, contacting known providers, establishing ownership or valuing an asset. No search can guarantee that every institution or product has been checked.

A search result is a lead to investigate, not proof that money belongs to the estate. For the practical steps involved in tracing and verifying different assets, see our [guide to finding a deceased person's assets](/blog/find-assets-deceased-person).

## When might further searching be useful?

Consider extra enquiries if one or more of these apply:

- Financial paperwork, email records or account statements are missing or incomplete.
- The person used several institutions or lived at earlier addresses that may still appear on old records.
- Past employment suggests a pension arrangement, but the scheme or provider is unclear.
- An old savings certificate, investment reference or unexplained transfer points to a holding that has not been accounted for.
- Family members, an accountant or a solicitor mention an account that is absent from the estate inventory.

These are reasons to investigate, not evidence that an undiscovered asset exists. Start with the specific clue and record what each enquiry establishes. If the question concerns a bank account after a death, our [guide to bank accounts after death](/blog/bank-account-when-someone-dies) explains why identifying an account and gaining access to its funds are separate matters.

## When might a paid search be unnecessary?

You may not need one if you have a reliable inventory, recent records for the relevant accounts and written confirmation from known institutions. A single missing account with an identifiable provider may be better handled by contacting that provider directly. Free, product-specific tracing may also answer a defined question. Reassess if those enquiries leave material gaps; a negative response from one service does not clear every type of asset.

### Free enquiries to try first

- **Known banks, building societies and other providers:** Contact their bereavement teams directly where records identify the institution. Ask what evidence of death and authority they require, and whether they can confirm holdings and date-of-death values. [GOV.UK's asset and debt guidance](https://www.gov.uk/valuing-estate-of-someone-who-died/identify-assets-debts) recommends identifying organisations from the person's papers and contacting them for values.
- **[My Lost Account](https://www.mylostaccount.org.uk/start-search):** This free service traces lost UK personal bank, building society and NS&I accounts. It accepts searches concerning someone who has died, but it is not a search of every active account, pension, investment or overseas holding. If you know the provider, approach it directly first.
- **[NS&I's tracing service](https://www.nsandi.com/help/lost-touch-with-nsandi/track-lost-investments):** This can check NS&I savings or investments, including where a deceased relative may have held them. It does not search other providers. NS&I says someone tracing on another person's behalf must be legally entitled to act, for example as an executor.
- **[GOV.UK's Pension Tracing Service](https://www.gov.uk/find-pension-contact-details):** This finds contact details for a workplace or personal pension scheme when you know an employer or provider name. It does not confirm whether a pension exists or its value. Contact the scheme to ask about any entitlement and its requirements. [MoneyHelper's guidance on pensions after death](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/family-and-care/death-and-bereavement/what-to-do-about-someones-pension-when-theyve-died) explains that pension benefits need separate attention.

## What should an executor or administrator record?

Personal representatives need to identify and deal with estate assets. [GOV.UK explains their responsibilities](https://www.gov.uk/probate-estate) and the need to identify assets and debts when valuing an estate. This does not amount to a blanket requirement to buy a commercial search. Keep a simple enquiry log: the clue, institution or service contacted, date, reference, documents supplied, response and next action. Keep copies of confirmations and distinguish an estimated value from a provider's confirmed date-of-death value.

Tell each institution your role and ask which documents it needs before it will disclose information or release funds. Requirements differ; a search result itself does not give authority to access an account. If ownership, debts, tax treatment or a disputed entitlement is unclear, seek appropriate professional advice rather than treating a match as a settled estate asset.

## What can a professional search result actually mean?

- **Possible match:** An institution has found information that may relate to the deceased; identity, ownership and value still need checking.
- **Confirmed asset:** The provider has verified a holding and supplied enough information to establish what it is. The amount payable to the estate may still need separate confirmation.
- **No match:** A responding institution found no matching record within the enquiry it performed. This does not rule out holdings elsewhere.
- **No response:** An enquiry is still outstanding or no usable answer has been received. It must not be counted as a negative result.
- **Completed search:** The agreed enquiries have reached the report's stated completion point. It does not mean every possible asset has been found.

Ask how the search provider uses these terms and how unresolved enquiries appear in its report.

## Questions to ask before paying for a search

1. Which asset types, institutions and geographic areas are included? Which are excluded?
2. Does the provider contact each named institution, and how does participation or non-response affect coverage?
3. What proof of identity, death and authority is needed, and who must supply it?
4. Will the report separate possible matches, confirmed holdings, negative responses and outstanding enquiries?
5. What follow-up is included if an institution replies later or asks for more documents?
6. What is the fee, what could cost extra, and what happens if no assets are identified?

Compare the answers with the gaps you actually need to resolve. ## Choose the next step that fits the gap

- **Known institution and enough identifying details:** Contact it directly and request its bereavement requirements.
- **Lead for a lost account or named pension scheme:** Try the relevant free tracing service, then follow up with the provider.
- **Incomplete records across several possible providers:** Define the missing asset types and compare the coverage and limits of a paid search.
- **Disputed or complex ownership, entitlement, debts or tax treatment:** Seek advice from a suitably qualified professional before distributing funds.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do executors have to use an asset-search company?

No general rule requires a professional search for every estate. The important task is to make sensible enquiries in light of the information available, identify assets and debts, and keep a record of what remains unresolved.

### Will a free lost-account search find every account?

No. My Lost Account is aimed at lost UK personal bank, building society and NS&I accounts. It cannot replace direct contact with a known provider or enquiries about other asset types.

### Can I search before a grant of probate is issued?

You can begin gathering records and asking institutions what they require. A provider may ask for different evidence before disclosing details or releasing funds. [GOV.UK's steps before applying for probate](https://www.gov.uk/applying-for-probate/before-you-apply) include estimating the estate's value; ask each institution what it will accept in your circumstances.

### Does a result mean the estate can claim the money?

No. The institution must verify the person and the holding, and the legal or beneficial entitlement may need checking. Pension benefits and joint holdings require particular care. Do not distribute funds based only on a possible match.

### What if a search returns no matches?

Review the institutions and asset types covered, the details supplied and any unanswered enquiries. A no-match result within a defined search is not proof that the deceased held no other assets. Follow up credible clues directly.
